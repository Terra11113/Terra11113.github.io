## Project Overview

This undergraduate thesis project explored chromatic-aberration-enhanced metalens design for compact spectral confocal displacement sensing. The motivation was to investigate whether a metasurface element could replace part of a traditional multi-lens dispersion structure while keeping wavelength-dependent focusing behavior useful for axial sensing.

**Role:** Undergraduate thesis project  
**Period:** 2024.3 - 2024.10  
**Keywords:** metalens, metasurface, spectral confocal sensing, chromatic aberration, FDTD Lumerical, MATLAB, geometric phase, optical-field simulation

## Technical Focus

- Derived wavelength-dependent phase profiles from Fermat's principle, generalized Snell's law, and geometric phase theory.
- Designed nanofin rotation rules for Pancharatnam-Berry phase modulation and metasurface phase control.
- Used FDTD Lumerical to screen meta-atom structures and analyze phase, transmittance, and optical-field responses.
- Studied RGB wavelength separation, focal-position shift, lateral spot distribution, and the relationship between structural parameters and energy concentration.

## My Role

I was responsible for the main simulation and analysis workflow, including phase-profile derivation, meta-atom parameter screening, array-rotation modeling, and MATLAB-assisted visualization. This project helped me build a practical connection between optical design theory and numerical simulation.

## Outcome

This project strengthened my foundation in computational optics, metasurface simulation, and optical-field analysis. It also gave me early research experience in turning physical optical principles into a simulation workflow with interpretable visual outputs.
